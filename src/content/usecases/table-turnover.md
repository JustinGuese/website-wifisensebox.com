### The Problem: The "Blind Spot" in Restaurant Operations

Most restaurants, especially large or multi-room establishments, suffer from a persistent operational "blind spot." The host at the front stand is often disconnected from the actual status of the dining room. They rely on servers to manually update the system or on periodic "walk-throughs" to see which tables have cleared. 

This manual process is fraught with inefficiency:
- **The Lag Time:** A table might sit empty and clean for five to ten minutes before the host even knows it's available. In a busy shift, those ten-minute gaps across twenty tables add up to hours of lost seating time.
- **The "Check-Back" Distraction:** Servers are busy. Expecting them to prioritize updating a tablet every time a guest leaves is unrealistic. Often, the update only happens after the table is already reset, leading to unnecessary delays at the front door.
- **Privacy vs. Visibility:** You could install cameras to monitor the floor, but guests hate being watched while they eat. Cameras in a dining room are a major privacy concern and a significant liability in the age of GDPR.
- **Inaccurate Wait Times:** When the host doesn't have real-time data, they give guests estimated wait times based on "gut feeling." If the estimate is too long, guests leave. If it's too short, they get frustrated. Both outcomes hurt your brand.

### Technical Deep Dive: The Physics of Occupancy Sensing

How can WiFi "see" a person sitting at a table? The technology relies on a principle called Channel State Information (CSI).

**The Traditional Failure (PIR):** Passive Infrared sensors are the most common occupancy sensors. They look for heat moving across their field of view. If a guest is sitting relatively still, enjoying a glass of wine, a PIR sensor will often time out and report the table as empty. This "false negative" makes them useless for restaurant management.

**The WifiSenseBox Advantage (CSI + Radar):**
- **Multipath Analysis:** WiFi signals travel from a transmitter to a receiver along multiple paths, bouncing off walls, floors, and furniture. When a human body (which is 70% water) enters that field, it disrupts those paths in a specific, measurable way.
- **Micro-Doppler Sensing:** WifiSenseBox uses radar-like processing to detect micro-movements, such as the rise and fall of a chest during breathing. Even if a guest is perfectly still, the system knows they are there.
- **Zone Definition:** Our algorithms allow you to define "zones" with sub-meter precision. You can isolate Table 4 from Table 5, even if they are only 18 inches apart.
- **Signal Filtering:** The system is smart enough to filter out "noise" like fans, swinging doors, or passing servers, focusing only on the stationary or semi-stationary presence of the guests at the table.
