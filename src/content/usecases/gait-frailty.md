In the world of geriatric medicine, gait—the way you walk—is increasingly recognized as the "sixth vital sign." It is a powerful predictor of future health outcomes, including the risk of falling, cognitive decline, and overall mortality. A steady, confident stride is a sign of a well-coordinated system between the brain, nerves, and muscles. Conversely, subtle changes in walking patterns are often the very first indicators that something is wrong.

The problem is that these changes are usually so gradual that they are invisible to the naked eye. Family members who see their loved ones every day don't notice the slight shortening of a stride or the extra second it takes to stand up from a chair. By the time a decline becomes obvious, the risk of a catastrophic fall has already reached critical levels. WifiSenseBox bridges this gap, providing a high-definition, continuous look at mobility that human observation simply cannot match.

## The Blind Spot of Modern Care: Why Intermittent Testing Fails

Current methods for assessing frailty and mobility are fundamentally flawed because they are intermittent and artificial.

### The "Snapshot" Problem
A typical mobility assessment involves a "Timed Up and Go" (TUG) test in a doctor's office or a physical therapy clinic. While useful, this is a single data point in a sterile environment. Many seniors experience "white coat syndrome," performing better in the clinic than they do in their own homes. Or, they may be having a particularly "good day" that doesn't reflect their typical evening fatigue or morning stiffness.

### The Subjectivity of Observation
Caregivers and family members rely on subjective observations: "Mom seems a bit slower today." But what does "a bit slower" actually mean? Without objective data, it's impossible to know if a decline is a temporary fluctuation or a sustained trend that requires medical intervention.

### The Crisis-Driven Model
In our current system, we usually only look at mobility *after* a crisis—following a fall, a hip fracture, or a stroke. We are perpetually reactive, trying to rebuild what has already been lost. WifiSenseBox shifts the paradigm to predictive care, allowing us to intervene while the senior is still mobile and independent.

## Technical Deep Dive: How WiFi "Sees" Your Stride

How can a WiFi signal distinguish between a healthy walk and a shuffle? The answer is in the complex interaction between radio waves and human biomechanics.

### Spatiotemporal Gait Parameters
As you walk, your body creates a rhythmic disturbance in the WiFi signals. WifiSenseBox's AI interprets these disturbances to calculate key metrics:
- **Gait Speed:** The time taken to cover a known distance within the sensing field.
- **Cadence:** The number of steps taken per minute.
- **Stride Length:** The distance between successive points of initial contact of the same foot.
- **Variability:** The degree of inconsistency in these measurements, which is a key indicator of fall risk.

### The Physics of Human RF Reflection
Human tissue is primarily composed of water, which reflects and absorbs RF signals in specific ways. When you walk, different parts of your body (arms, legs, torso) move at different velocities. This creates a complex "point cloud" of reflections. WifiSenseBox uses advanced signal processing to isolate the movement of the legs from the rest of the body, allowing for the calculation of step-level data.

### Machine Learning for Frailty Scoring
We don't just provide raw numbers. WifiSenseBox uses proprietary machine learning models, trained on clinical datasets of senior movement, to generate a "Frailty Index." This index combines multiple gait parameters to provide a single, easy-to-understand score that correlates with established geriatric scales.
