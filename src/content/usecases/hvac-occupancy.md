## The Failure of Traditional Occupancy Sensors

For years, the industry has relied on Passive Infrared (PIR) sensors. We’ve all experienced their limitations: the lights suddenly clicking off while you’re deep in concentration at your desk, forcing you to wave your arms like a stranded traveler to get the system’s attention.

While PIR sensors are better than nothing, they are fundamentally flawed for HVAC control:

1.  **The "Stillness" Problem:** PIR sensors detect movement, not presence. If a person is sitting still—reading, typing, or in a focused meeting—the sensor fails. This leads to "false negatives," where the system thinks a room is empty when it isn't.
2.  **Line-of-Sight Limitations:** PIR sensors require a direct line of sight. Cubicle walls, monitors, and even office plants can create blind spots, rendering the sensor useless in complex office layouts.
3.  **Slow Response Times:** Because of the high rate of false negatives, facility managers often set long "time-out" delays (often 15 to 30 minutes) before the HVAC or lighting scales back. This means you are still wasting significant energy every time someone leaves a room.
4.  **Maintenance Headaches:** Battery-operated PIR sensors require constant monitoring and replacement, adding to the operational burden of the facility team.

## Technical Deep Dive: The Power of CSI (Channel State Information)

To understand how WifiSenseBox outperforms every other sensor on the market, we have to look under the hood at Channel State Information (CSI).

### What is CSI?
Every time a WiFi signal travels from a transmitter to a receiver, it interacts with the environment. It bounces off walls, passes through furniture, and reflects off human bodies. These interactions alter the signal in very specific ways. CSI is a digital record of these alterations across the entire frequency spectrum of the WiFi channel.

### The Breathing Signature
Human bodies are composed mostly of water, which makes them highly reflective to WiFi signals. More importantly, the act of breathing causes the chest cavity to expand and contract. This tiny movement—just a few millimeters—creates a distinct, rhythmic "shimmer" in the CSI data. 

WifiSenseBox uses proprietary machine learning algorithms to isolate this shimmer from background noise (like a spinning fan or a passing car). By identifying the frequency of this rhythmic change, WifiSenseBox can distinguish between a human being and a mechanical object with incredible precision.

### Through-Wall Capability
Because WiFi signals naturally penetrate common building materials like drywall, glass, and wood, a single WifiSenseBox unit can often monitor an entire suite of offices or a large conference room without needing a clear line of sight. This reduces the number of devices required per square foot, lowering the total cost of ownership (TCO) and simplifying installation.
